The Risk Management Lifecycle

Core Process
Key Concept: Risk management is an iterative, continuous loop—not a static compliance checkbox. As infrastructure and threat actors evolve, the risk posture must be continually reassessed.

1. Context & Scope

Establish organizational objectives, external legal mandates, regulatory compliance rules, and executive risk tolerance boundaries.

2. Risk Identification

Catalog information assets, profile active threat agents, discover system vulnerabilities, and document current safeguards.

3. Risk Analysis

Estimate likelihood of occurrence and assess financial and operational impact through qualitative ratings or mathematical models.

4. Risk Evaluation

Benchmark analyzed risk scores against predetermined risk acceptance criteria to prioritize remediation urgency.

5. Risk Treatment

Formulate and execute response strategies: Mitigate (Controls), Avoid (Terminate), Transfer (Insure), or Retain (Accept).

6. Monitor & Audit

Continuously inspect control integrity, track zero-day vulnerabilities, log security telemetry, and communicate to stakeholders.

Information Asset Identification & Valuation

Asset Governance

Asset Taxonomy (What We Defend)

  • Data & Intellectual Property: Student transcripts, faculty payroll, research algorithms, source code.
  • Software & Platforms: Student information systems (SIS), ERP, cloud endpoints, operating systems.
  • Hardware & Network: Core servers, edge switches, perimeter firewalls, fiber backbones, Wi-Fi APs.
  • Human Capital: Database administrators, campus security personnel, developers, faculty.
  • Physical Facilities: University server rooms, cooling systems, generator backups.

Asset Governance Principles

  • 1. Inventory Discovery: Automated network probes and Configuration Management Database (CMDB) tracking.
  • 2. Explicit Ownership: Every asset must have a designated senior owner accountable for its protection and access approvals.
  • 3. Data Classification:
    Public Internal Confidential Restricted
  • 4. Dependency Mapping: Identify downstream cascade risks (e.g., Auth Server downtime halts all exam logins).

Risk Mechanics: Threats, Vulnerabilities & Impacts

Formulation
Security Risk = Threat Vector × Vulnerability × Asset Impact

Threat Vectors

  • Human: Social engineering, phishing, rogue insiders, credential theft.
  • Technical: Zero-day exploits, ransomware, botnets, DDoS saturation.
  • Environmental: Power grid blackouts, water leaks, severe flooding.
  • Supply Chain: Compromised dependencies, untrusted vendor code.

Vulnerabilities

  • Software: Unpatched libraries, SQL injection, insecure deserialization.
  • Configuration: Default administrative credentials, exposed SSH/RDP ports.
  • Architectural: Single points of failure, missing network isolation.
  • Policy: Absent MFA enforcement, irregular backup verification.

Impact Dimensions

  • Confidentiality: Unauthorized exfiltration of student identities.
  • Integrity: Malicious tampering of final examination GPA scores.
  • Availability: Severe portal crash during nationwide admission intake.
  • Legal & Financial: Statutory penalties, lawsuits, institutional reputational damage.

Risk Assessment Methodologies

Analysis Models

Qualitative Analysis (Matrix)

Fast, subjective evaluation using defined ranking categories.

Likelihood \ ImpactMinor (1)Moderate (2)Severe (3)
High (3)Medium (3)High (6)Critical (9)
Medium (2)Low (2)Medium (4)High (6)
Low (1)Low (1)Low (2)Medium (3)

*Scores ≥ 6 require urgent engineering mitigation and executive sign-off.

Quantitative Analysis (Financial ROI)

SLE = AV × EF (Single Loss Expectancy)
ALE = SLE × ARO (Annualized Loss Expectancy)

Concrete Numerical Example:

  • Central Database Value (AV) = 1,200,000 BDT
  • Exposure Factor (EF) under Ransomware = 25%
  • SLE = 1,200,000 × 0.25 = 300,000 BDT
  • Annual Rate of Occurrence (ARO) = 0.2 (Once in 5 years)
  • ALE = 300,000 × 0.2 = 60,000 BDT / year

Decision Rule: Any security countermeasure cost must not exceed 60,000 BDT annually.

Strategic Risk Treatment Options

Remediation

1. Risk Mitigation (Reduction)

Applying technical and managerial countermeasures to lower the vulnerability exposure or impact severity.

Example: Enforcing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), automated vulnerability patching, and segmented firewalls.

2. Risk Transference (Sharing)

Transferring the potential financial burden to an external insurer or cloud provider under rigorous Service Level Agreements.

Example: Procuring cyber breach insurance; shifting data storage to AWS with uptime indemnification.

3. Risk Avoidance (Termination)

Discontinuing or redesigning the business activity that introduces the hazard when risks exceed strategic tolerance.

Example: Decommissioning legacy FTP servers; disabling unencrypted remote desktop protocols over public internet.

4. Risk Acceptance (Retention)

Formally retaining residual risk when treatment costs exceed asset value and risk falls comfortably within corporate appetite.

Example: Documenting and accepting occasional harmless UI error notifications in non-critical internal apps.

Defense-in-Depth: Security Controls Taxonomy

Protection Layers

Functional Taxonomy (Timeline of Intervention)

Preventive Controls

Stops breaches before they occur. Examples: MFA, AES-256 data encryption, input validation, role-based access.

Detective Controls

Identifies intrusions as they happen. Examples: SIEM logging, Network Intrusion Detection (NIDS), CCTV, anomaly alarms.

Corrective Controls

Remediates damage and recovers operations. Examples: Disaster recovery hot-sites, backup restoration, automated patch push.

Mechanism Taxonomy (Nature of Realization)

Technical Controls

Firewalls, cryptographic certificates, antimalware agents, automated honeypots.

Administrative Controls

Acceptable Use Policies, background checks, mandatory security awareness campaigns.

Physical Controls

Biometric turnstiles, reinforced server vaults, fire suppression gases, backup diesel generators.

Information Security Governance

Strategic Oversight
Definition: Security Governance is the framework of strategic leadership, organizational accountability, and oversight through which executive leadership directs and safeguards the organization's information assets.

Governance (Board & Executive Leadership)

  • Sets Direction: Aligns security investments with business mission and vision.
  • Approves Risk Appetite: Formally signs off on accepted risk tolerance levels.
  • Allocates Resources: Authorizes cyber defense capital expenditure and staffing.
  • Ensures Compliance: Guarantees fidelity to ISO standards, GDPR, and privacy laws.

Management (Security Operations & IT)

  • Executes Strategy: Deploys technical configurations, firewalls, and IAM policies.
  • Maintains Daily Controls: Performs security monitoring, patching, and backups.
  • Incident Handling: Runs CSIRT incident response playbooks during cyberattacks.
  • Performance Reporting: Supplies MTTD, MTTR, and patch compliance metrics to executives.

ISMS & The Deming PDCA Cycle (ISO/IEC 27001)

Management Systems

An Information Security Management System (ISMS) provides a repeatable blueprint for governing organizational risk. The Deming PDCA cycle ensures continual security maturity:

PLAN

Context & Risk:

  • Define ISMS boundaries
  • Identify threats and assets
  • Select treatment controls
  • Draft Statement of Applicability

DO

Implementation:

  • Deploy chosen controls
  • Allocate security budgets
  • Train administrative staff
  • Enforce credential rules

CHECK

Audit & Review:

  • Conduct internal audits
  • Inspect firewall log data
  • Track vulnerability metrics
  • Executive review meetings

ACT

Continual Improvement:

  • Remediate audit findings
  • Update defensive baselines
  • Refine security policies
  • Enhance control resilience

Security Documentation Hierarchy

Policies to Procedures
POLICY
High-Level Strategic Directive (Mandatory — What & Why)
Example: "All university computing assets must enforce strict access authorization to protect student confidentiality."
STANDARD
Specific Mandatory Baseline (Mandatory — The Rule & Threshold)
Example: "Passwords must be at least 14 characters, require MFA, and databases must use AES-256 encryption."
PROCEDURE
Step-by-Step Tactical Workflow (Mandatory — How to Execute)
Example: "Step 1: Receive HR separation ticket. Step 2: Disable AD login within 60 mins. Step 3: Revoke VPN tokens."
GUIDELINE
Advisory Best Practices (Discretionary — Recommendations)
Example: "Faculty working remotely are recommended to position laptop screens away from public view in coffee shops."

Security Evaluation & Assurance

Testing & Verification

Vulnerability Assessment (VA)

Breadth over Depth (Automated)

  • Automated scanning using Nessus, OpenVAS, or Qualys.
  • Identifies missing software patches, default ports, and known CVE bugs.
  • Delivers a broad, prioritized inventory of weak points for rapid remediation.

Penetration Testing (Pen-Test)

Depth over Breadth (Authorized Attack)

  • Certified ethical hackers simulate real-world Advanced Persistent Threats (APT).
  • Actively exploits flaws, chains vulnerabilities, and tests lateral movement.
  • Conducted as Black-Box, Gray-Box, or White-Box engagements.

Security Audits & Assurance

Compliance & Process Verification

  • Formal, evidence-based assessment against standards (ISO 27001, SOC 2).
  • Verifies whether policies are actively enforced rather than mere shelf-ware.
  • Tracks key operational KPIs: Mean Time to Detect (MTTD) and Patch Compliance %.

End-to-End Case Study: University Examination Portal

Real-World Integration

1. Asset & Owner

Asset: Central Examination & Grade Database.

Owner: Controller of Examinations, JUST.

Classification: Highly Confidential / Restricted.

2. Threat & Vulnerability

Threat: Rogue actors attempting SQL Injection or credential stuffing.

Vulnerability: Missing input sanitization & faculty portal lacked mandatory MFA.

3. Risk Rating

Likelihood: Moderate (3) | Impact: Critical (5)

Score: 15 / 25 (High Risk Priority).

Unacceptable risk requiring immediate executive mitigation.

4. Treatment Deployed

Mitigate: Parameterized SQL, enforced FIDO2 MFA, deployed Web Application Firewall (WAF).

Transfer: Automated immutable cloud backup with 4-hour RTO SLA.

5. Governance Alignment

Policy: Grade Submission & Academic Integrity Policy.

Standard: Minimum 16-character passkeys, AES-256 DB encryption.

Procedure: Dual-faculty signoff for grade edits.

6. Assurance & Audit

Quarterly external black-box pen-testing conducted prior to semester finals.

Continuous SIEM log monitoring for abnormal grade modification spikes.

Summary & Key Takeaways for CLO2

Core Takeaways

1. Asset Valuation Precedes Protection

You cannot defend what you do not understand or value. Clear asset ownership and classification form the bedrock of cybersecurity.

2. Risk is a Calculated Balance

Security risk blends threat vectors, exploitability, and organizational loss. Both qualitative speed and quantitative financial formulas (ALE) are required.

3. Treatment Requires Deliberate Choice

Organizations must strategically select between mitigation, transference, avoidance, and documented acceptance while tracking residual risk.

4. Governance Sets the Direction

Governance directs accountability and strategy; operational management handles implementation; ISMS (ISO 27001) makes the system repeatable.
